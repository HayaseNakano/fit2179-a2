All states have seen income growth since 1970, but KL's
median income grew ~40× while Kelantan's grew only ~18×.
Terengganu climbed dramatically from rank 10 to rank 5 —
driven by oil revenue. Gini coefficients remain stubbornly
high in Sabah and Kelantan, indicating persistent internal
inequality even within poorer states. The mean-median gap
reveals that in high-income states, a small number of very
wealthy households pull the average far above what most
residents actually earn.

expectancy. Yet income inequality persists — economic growth
has not been equally shared across all states and
